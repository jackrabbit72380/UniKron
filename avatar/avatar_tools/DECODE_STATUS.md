## Shape overrides (block 4)

**266 assets.** None of them carries a model, a texture or an
animation of its own: each is a morph onto someone else's mesh, which is why
they cannot be drawn alone whatever the reader does.

The block is a bitstream of two sections, each `count, u32, 16-byte record,
base, width`, then a bit-packed list. Geometry shapes have an empty first
section; clothing hiding templates carry their vertex list there. The second
section holds 32-byte entries, and an entry is a vertex and that vertex's
new position: the leading field is a byte offset, so the vertex is the
offset divided by the 52-byte vertex stride, and the three floats are the
position, quantised at 7.071e-06 per unit.

Section A is a plain 12-bit index list for the clothing templates and empty
for the geometry shapes, and each section header is byte-aligned - the
padding after an odd-length list is what made eight of them read their
second header four bits early.

All 266 decode and every payload is consumed to within a byte. For the
facial shapes the positions land millimetres from the vertices they name and
on the anatomy the item is called after. One thing is not read: the
meaning of the four trailing pairs in each entry (lengths right, values
not).

Each shape targets a stock mesh, and verify_shapes.py checks that rather
than assuming it: 207 of the 215 items with entries put every entry within
60 mm of the vertex its offset names - 33 on Head, 84 on MaleBody, 90 on
FemaleBody, mean 8 mm - and three of the high heels sit on FemaleBody with a
few vertices further out, which is what lifting a foot does.

## Faults in the artwork itself

87 hairstyles are scalp caps, not styles: the top of the mesh sits
0.128 to 0.166 below the crown of the head, against -0.104 to +0.096
for every full style. Each is named "(Hat)" and is the scalp worn
under a hat, so offered on its own it reads as bald. This was
confirmed against the baked sets, not inferred from the names:

- Peak (Hat) (0.166 below the crown, 94 verts)
